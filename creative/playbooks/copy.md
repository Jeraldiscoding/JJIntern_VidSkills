# Copy that earns its next sentence

Write for a Singapore student who is busy and sceptical. Name the task, constraint or discovery. Use “internship”, “company”, “role”, “requirements” and “apply” when those are the actual actions. Do not force Singlish or use US-specific campus language without reason.

| Avoid | Use when supported | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Unlock your potential with the ultimate career platform. | Know the company? Search it on JJIntern. | Observable action and a clear starting point. |
| Your dream internship is one click away. | Find a company, then check its current roles. | No guaranteed outcome or invented one-click application. |
| Stop wasting your life on job boards. | Which tab was that company in? | Recognisable situation without insulting the viewer. |
| This secret internship pays S$12.5k! | Check the role, location and pay period before comparing pay. | No hearsay, concealment claim or unsupported compensation. |
| Only 24 hours left! | Applications close [verified date and timezone]. | Use the second only after checking the employer. |
| We track every internship in Singapore. | Browse companies on JJIntern. | Coverage and completeness need evidence. |
| No experience? You’re guaranteed a shot. | The posting lists no prior experience requirement. | Quote the exact evidence without predicting admission. |
| Land quant. Effortlessly. | Read the eligibility before you shortlist a quant role. | Useful next step; no false ease. |

Brackets above illustrate a required evidence field, not publishable copy. Final briefs must contain exact finished text or an explicit blocked shot.

## Hook contract

Write the payoff beside each hook. “Know the company?” promises finding that company, not a tutorial on a different feature. “GIC: beyond finance degrees?” promises the employer's subject eligibility rule and its conditions. If the proof disappoints the opening, change the opening.

Use one claim per reading beat, roughly 5–10 words for the opening and 8–16 for an explanatory beat. These are editorial starting points; read aloud and time the real layout. Put essential qualifications next to the claim, not only in a caption.

## Evidence and compensation

Use `claims/template.json` for employer, exact role, location, currency, pay period, base/total, estimate/employer-confirmed, programme year, source, checked date and mandatory qualifications. Unknown is a value, not permission to omit context. A missing essential compensation field blocks the number. Do not annualise a monthly stipend unless the purpose and arithmetic are explicit; never present that calculation as placement earnings.

The owner's anecdote about a friend earning S$12.5k at Temasek is **unverified**. It cannot become a hook, benchmark, testimonial or pay range. Do not use an anonymised “friend” story to bypass this rule.

Use product labels exactly in a demo. If interface text is awkward, retain it in the capture and make the surrounding editorial copy clear. Avoid manufacturing social proof, student counts, results, speed or employer relationships.

## Salary and company-list hooks

Use the [career content playbook](career-content.md) for worked hooks, visible qualifications and body/CTA structure. Keep the exciting fact at the front; evidence discipline should sharpen it, not replace every salary story with a generic warning about pay.

“Best” must answer “best for whom, by what measure?” For an editorial selection, prefer “Three tech companies for your SWE shortlist” and deliver a concrete reason for each. Use “highest-paying” only with a defined comparison set and compatible evidence. Do not add “secret,” “guaranteed,” “easy” or “hiring now” for intensity unless the exact assertion is supported.
