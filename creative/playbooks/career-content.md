## Creative principle

Start from a verified JJIntern destination and an employer-specific reason that makes visiting it useful. Lead with information a student would want: a precise compensation detail, distinctive work, a relevant programme or an eligibility fact. Give enough decision value for the student to understand the opportunity, then make continuing discovery on JJIntern the natural next step. One website action should connect the body, final panel and caption.

The owner's latest preference is **short, clean and informative**: one dominant fact per picture, few supporting words and one website CTA for the sequence. Remove repeated explanations before reducing type size; keep material qualifications readable beside the claim. Vary panel structures rather than turning each company into the same generic checklist. Pictures need no soundtrack, timing or animation. If the owner later requests an actual video, adapt the sequence with the [video workflow](video.md); preserve the claim context.

Verify employer claims in current primary sources before writing finished copy. A company programme page can establish programme details; a specific employer listing can establish its published location, eligibility and compensation. Neither establishes that the role is present on JJIntern. Verify that separately. If compensation cannot be supported, use a stronger sourced non-pay hook instead of inventing an amount or repeating an anecdote.

## 1. Salary spotlight: the number has a story

**Promise:** explain a surprising employer/role compensation figure and what it means for the relevant student. One employer and one clearly scoped figure are usually enough.

**Opening ingredients:** recognisable employer + exact role + compelling amount + visible currency/period/location/reporting context. Split across a headline and a readable adjacent context line. If that cannot fit, simplify the hook rather than shrink essential facts. Material qualifications must be visible on the picture that carries the number, not saved for a later swipe or caption.

Useful hook structures, to populate only after verification:

- A direct reveal: employer, role and qualified pay in the first composition; the next beat answers “for which internship?”
- A question resolved by evidence: “What does a software engineering intern earn here?” Keep it specific enough that the reveal is worth waiting for.
- A local comparison with a clear basis: comparable Singapore roles and pay periods, with each employer's reporting context visible.
- A detail-first discovery: “The pay is only half the story.” Resolve with a sourced responsibility, eligibility condition or compensation component.

**Flexible swipe structure:** choose panel count by the evidence and decision value, not a fixed template. These are proposed editorial jobs, not a reconstruction of an inspected TikTok.

| Panel job | Viewer receives | Visual job |
|---|---|---|
| Reveal | Employer, role and qualified figure | One strong typographic composition; number and unit stay together |
| Establish and assess | Source basis plus useful role or eligibility detail | Authentic source excerpt or clear attribution; highlight the decision-relevant fact |
| Connect | Why this employer belongs in the student's search, and what JJIntern can actually help them do next | A concise comparison, genuine destination capture or specific discovery instruction |
| Act | One useful website action and checked destination | A legible CTA; the caption reinforces the same action |

A salary hook needs defensible Singapore evidence for the same employer and role. Distinguish employer-published pay for the featured intake from a crowd-reported local benchmark. Say “reported” where a reported number appears; show its source and sample size. When report dates are undisclosed, an otherwise relevant benchmark may be used cautiously with **“dates undisclosed”** and **“current intake pay unconfirmed”** beside the amount. The date you checked a page is not the date its salaries were reported. Do not call an undated report current, guaranteed or “likely” without additional evidence. Do not invent a range or combine incompatible reports into one.

Preserve the original pay period. Annualised rates are not total internship earnings. Keep base pay, bonuses, housing and other benefits separate unless a defensible total is explicitly explained; do not label an unspecified amount as base pay. Use local SGD evidence, never overseas pay, currency conversions or another role as a Singapore salary proxy. Comparisons need compatible roles, periods, locations and reporting bases; show any necessary period calculation. If the local role or amount cannot be established, choose another source or a non-pay hook.

**Historical worked example, checked 24 October 2026; already covered, not the next post:** [Stripe's Singapore Software Engineer Intern listing](https://stripe.com/careers/listing/software-engineer-intern/8130883) lists an annual SGD 72,000–72,000 salary range. A possible opening is **“Stripe SWE intern: S$6k/month equivalent”**, with **“Singapore · S$72,000/year ÷ 12”** beside it on the cover. The calculation is `72,000 ÷ 12 = 6,000`; it does not establish actual monthly payments or total internship earnings. Explain the annual basis in the body and caption, use claim `E02-STRIPE-SG-SWE-2026-10-24`, and recheck the source before release. The linked employer application form supplies the 2027 intake context, as recorded in the claim ledger. No deadline or guarantee of continued availability has been established; refresh the application destination before any “hiring now” wording.

**Payoff failures:** a big number followed only by a JJIntern logo; an unreadable source/qualification; revealing currency or location after a misleading cover; a “salary comparison” that mixes years or base and total compensation; a final CTA unrelated to what the student just learned.

## 2. Company shortlist: give each name a reason

**Promise:** help a defined student build a more useful research list. Specify the lens: software engineering, infrastructure, fintech, a particular location, or another evidenced interest. A single-company spotlight is appropriate for the current brief; for a multi-company carousel, choose the count by useful distinctions rather than name recognition alone.

Possible openings:

- “Three tech companies for your SWE shortlist.”
- “Your SWE shortlist needs more than famous names.”
- “Start your fintech internship research here.”
- “Best for what? Build a shortlist around the work.”

Treat these as proposed hooks, not finished briefs. The body must earn the chosen promise. Avoid a prestigious-name montage with the same vague praise on every entry.

**Possible shortlist structure:**

| Panel job | Viewer receives | Visual job |
|---|---|---|
| Lens | Who the list helps and why these companies belong together | Strong cover with the audience/criterion visible |
| Companies | Each name plus a distinct sourced reason to research it | Consistent hierarchy; vary composition around the evidence |
| Decision | A comparison that helps choose a search direction | Readable differences; combine with company panels when possible |
| Next step | The relevant JJIntern action | One clear CTA with a verified destination |

A good reason is concrete work, a verified programme feature, relevant eligibility or a clearly scoped compensation finding. “Great culture,” “best experience,” “easy to get into” and return-offer claims need their own evidence; a famous name does not establish them.

No current vacancy is required for an evergreen research shortlist. Avoid “hiring now” and “apply today” unless the exact applications have been checked. Use plain company names or authentic source/product crops; do not create employer logos or imply endorsement.

## 3. Ranking: make “best” answer a real question

Use a ranked list when a consistent criterion supports the order. Define the audience, geography, date, comparison set and method before writing “top,” “highest-paying” or “best.” Explain that scope where the viewer needs it; a ranking of a small reviewed set is not a ranking of every tech employer.

- For a numerical ranking, use comparable data and handle missing values and ties honestly. Do not infer low pay from missing reports.
- For an attributed external ranking, name the publisher, year and criterion. Its result is not JJIntern's independently verified universal verdict.
- For editorial judgement, label it as a shortlist or picks and explain the selection lens. Sequential numbers can organise the story without pretending to measure superiority.

Reveal reasons alongside names. A countdown can create anticipation, but the first entry must already offer value. Do not hold all useful information until number one. A split-screen comparison may serve the evidence better than a countdown.

## Make the website visit useful and honest

Check the exact destination and the promised action during production. A company-research piece can point to JJIntern's verified directory; a relevant role-discovery piece can point to the feed. Verify any claim that the specific employer, role or opportunity is available there. A directory tile is not an open role, and an employer vacancy does not prove JJIntern carries it. If a specific result is absent or cannot be verified, use an honest broader action such as exploring tech/finance internships; do not imply the featured opportunity is waiting on the site.

Give one clear action and a legible destination on the final picture; reinforce it in the caption. State the benefit the visitor can actually receive. “Link in bio” requires checking the account's real bio destination; without that evidence use the checked website address directly. Avoid stacked requests to save, follow, comment and sign up. Product demonstrations are optional; show only freshly verified behaviour and genuine captures when used.

Do not conceal material eligibility, pay conditions or the promised answer to force a click. Do not invent an exclusive list, salary tool, personalised recommendation or application service. The post should already be useful; visiting JJIntern should extend that usefulness. The student applies through the appropriate employer route. Include primary-source attribution and the check date in the caption/source record without pretending those sources endorse JJIntern.

## Pictures should make the answer and next action clear

- Give the cover one dominant fact, not a branded introduction. The employer and reason to care should be immediately clear.
- Keep amounts and units together. Use alignment or a source highlight to make context easier to understand.
- Preserve company/name/reason hierarchy so comparison gets easier with each entry. Change composition when the content changes: a bold fact, a qualification, a comparison and a website action have different visual needs.
- Give every panel standalone factual context. Never require the previous image to remain visible to interpret a salary or eligibility claim accurately.
- Use the existing brand type guidance and inspect every PNG at 360 px width. Essential qualifications start at 42 px on a 1080 px canvas; reduce copy or add a panel before shrinking them.
- Export numbered sRGB PNGs, a caption, per-panel alt text and a source manifest. Include an overview/contact sheet for local review when helpful; it does not replace individual pictures.

## How to inspect TikTok references

Start with relevant public original posts, including photo carousels, not a generic “viral video” compilation. For each candidate, record the URL, creator, publication date if visible, check date, format, topic and inspection scope. A title, thumbnail, search snippet or transcript can supply a lead but cannot establish the complete swipe sequence or playback.

For a carousel, inspect each accessible image in order and record the cover words/image, question, first useful answer, new information per swipe, hierarchy and qualifications, JJIntern-relevant product bridge, CTA and one technique to adapt. Note missing panels. For a playable video, additionally inspect motion/audio and record exact timings only when measured. If part of a post is unavailable, say so.

Separate three columns in the research note: **observed**, **interpretation**, **JJIntern adaptation**. Public view/like/comment counts are snapshots with a check date, not proof that a hook caused reach. Do not infer retention, conversion, organic distribution or audience relevance from a view count. Record access limits and continue with source research and concept work rather than claiming an inaccessible clip was watched.

Use references for mechanisms, not copied expression: amount-first information hierarchy, a useful reason per company, a clear comparison or a brief CTA. Keep the original script, visual execution and evidence specific to JJIntern's Singapore student audience.

Use actual platform swipe depth, last-panel reach or saves as supporting signals if available; do not invent them or substitute video completion metrics.
