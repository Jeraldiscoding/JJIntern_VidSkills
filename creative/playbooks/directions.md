# Eight different ways to tell a JJIntern story

These are territories, not reusable slide layouts. Read [owner preferences](../CREATIVE-DIRECTION.md) and product evidence before choosing one. Make sound a deliberate choice; every version must work muted. The initial five hooks below are proposals. Territory 6 has an accepted local execution reference. Territories 7–8 are the owner's 24 October 2026 priorities to test; none is a measured performance winner.

## 1. The unexpected job, examined

**Audience/problem:** students who only know the famous job titles or assume their subject rules them out. **Idea:** open on an unexpected requirement or responsibility, then reveal the employer evidence and actual eligibility.

**Hooks:** “GIC: beyond finance degrees?” / “Read the requirements before you rule it out.” Use the first only with the primary evidence and qualifications in brief B; never attach an unsupported salary or announce an unverified intake.

**Story:** surprising requirement/task → named employer/programme → source detail → who can apply → where to check current requirements. **Visual language:** editorial evidence desk: a large key phrase, a tight real employer-page crop, one annotation aligned to the relevant line. No employer logo recreation. One blue underline may connect claim to evidence.

**Motion/sound:** reveal source after the question, track along a responsibility line, hold long enough to read; one dry click on the reveal, silence on qualifications. **Product:** JJIntern introduces the discovery habit; show a JJIntern listing only if it actually exists. Otherwise keep employer application destination explicit and JJIntern as secondary discovery CTA. **CTA:** “Check the employer’s current requirements.” **Use:** a role with unusually concrete work and first-party evidence. **Failure:** “now hiring” from an old page, cropping eligibility, suggesting all disciplines qualify, making JJIntern the application provider without proof.

## 2. The tab that got away

**Audience/problem:** students who cannot remember which company they intended to look up. **Idea:** visualise the familiar search mess, then resolve one concrete search.

**Hooks:** “Which tab was that company in?” / “You remembered the company. Not the tab.” **Story:** generic unbranded tab labels compete → one remembered company name remains → cut into real directory search → correct company becomes visible → direct URL. **Visual language:** edge-to-edge typographic tab strip, asymmetric close crop, then real product surface. The opening is an editorial metaphor labelled as such in the asset record, never a fake JJIntern UI.

**Motion/sound:** tabs shuffle only while the viewer is trying to locate a name; hard stop when the name is entered into real search. Small paper shuffle or self-made taps, followed by a clean click. **Product:** verified company-directory search. No invented saved-tab, syncing or tracker function. **CTA:** “Find the company on JJIntern.com/companies.” **Use:** relatability and an easy first product action. **Failure:** shame about student organisation, 15 seconds of chaos before a product reveal, invented time saved, fake success notification.

## 3. One action, visible consequence

**Audience/problem:** students who do not know where to start on JJIntern. **Idea:** demonstrate the smallest useful journey with an observable before/after.

**Hooks:** “Know the company? Start here.” / “Find the company. Then check the roles.” **Story:** real directory → real input/action → visible filtered result → explain the boundary (“company result ≠ open vacancy”) → CTA. **Visual language:** actual mobile capture fills a stable viewing window; one editorial heading, no floating browser with decorative halo. Keep input and result in the same spatial frame.

**Motion/sound:** actual typed characters and disappearing nonmatches establish causality; crop once to improve legibility. No autonomous fake cursor. **Product:** only the tested action. Record event timing so a shortened edit does not claim measured product speed. **CTA:** “Try your company: JJIntern.com/companies.” **Use:** onboarding and conversion tests. **Failure:** stale screenshots, moving the camera during the result, listing skeletons presented as data, UI too small to read.

## 4. Read the fine print, keep the useful part

**Audience/problem:** students comparing roles with unclear eligibility or pay context. **Idea:** teach one reading skill with a decision students can reuse.

**Hooks:** “Before you compare internship pay, check the period.” / “A big number needs a unit.” **Story:** clearly hypothetical pay-format example → monthly versus annualised versus total placement distinction → what fields to check → saveable checklist. **Visual language:** large aligned units and a plain ruled comparison; no invented employer or salary. Prefer a manual carousel with a clear information gain per swipe.

**Motion/sound:** units align across a shared baseline; in video, replace one unit at a time rather than decorative counters. Muted comprehension must be complete. **Product:** genuine role descriptions can demonstrate where to look once availability is confirmed; otherwise JJIntern is the sign-off, with no unverified in-app salary comparison claim. **CTA:** “Save the checklist for your next role.” **Use:** useful education and trust. **Failure:** presenting annualised pay as summer earnings, using a prestigious employer for an unsourced number, excessive caveats in tiny type.

## 5. Search query confession

**Audience/problem:** students whose ambitious target list is still a vague plan. **Idea:** a small visual joke about the gap between intent and a concrete next action.

**Hooks:** “My internship plan: ‘figure it out’.” / “A plan needs at least one company name.” **Story:** a large editorial note “figure it out” → an intentionally awkward pause → note replaced by one company name → real directory search → “That’s a start.” **Visual language:** mostly neutral negative space serving the pause, one oversized line, then full product. No simulated chat message, fake student testimonial or generated person.

**Motion/sound:** one deletion, one deliberate entry, then real UI consequence; a self-recorded key tap can punctuate the pause. No bouncing emoji. **Product:** company search; honest payoff is a first action, not an internship offer. **CTA:** “Pick one company. Start on JJIntern.” **Use:** light humour among more informational posts. **Failure:** joke longer than payoff, smug tone, reinforcing anxiety, presenting a fictional quote as a real user.

## 6. Discovery made visible

**Audience/problem:** students want to spend their attention applying rather than repeatedly hunting for opportunities. **Idea:** express discovery as a coherent visual transformation and arrival. The mechanism can vary; the packet is one execution.

**Reference story:** a readable source becomes compact information → a sustained sense of speed → visible entry into JJIntern → short receipt → finding/applying payoff. **Visual language:** a few flat shapes, strong silhouette, original brand mark and ample clear space. The [accepted revision 11](../projects/discovery-packet/README.md) uses a pale-blue field and stationary foreground against a speeding background, then a centred capture.

**Motion/sound:** quick connected morph, deliberate travel interval, geometric contact/occlusion, brief stable result and calmer copy. A drum-led score carries momentum; dings mark source and receipt. Use current owner preferences, not the abandoned travelling packet, whip or drifting Found result. **Product:** an explicitly scoped editorial discovery metaphor. It is not a UI demonstration, live vacancy announcement or measured ingestion event. Any factual capability or employer claim still needs evidence. **Payoff:** “We handle the finding. / You focus on applying.” **Ending:** the reference has a brand-only signoff and a destination in its caption. **Use:** a designed brand film about the discovery/applying division. **Failure:** abstract movement with no understandable transformation, unrelated logo crossfade, excessive holding, uniformly rushed pacing, passive music or an implied instant-detection/auto-apply promise.

## 7. The pay number, explained

**Audience/problem:** Singapore students want concrete reasons to investigate a company. **Idea:** lead with one sourced employer/role pay figure, explain its basis, then add a useful role detail. The number is the discovery, not a promise of an offer.

**Story:** employer + qualified pay → inspectable source → relevant work or requirement → useful next step. **Visual language:** large number with an inseparable unit/context line, then evidence aligned to it. Use the [salary spotlight recipe](career-content.md) for hook options and timing. **Product:** a brief discovery signoff or verified destination after the value; a product demo is optional. **Failure:** unsourced pay, hidden annualisation, mixed locations/years, or an oversized number that leads only to a logo.

## 8. Build your tech-company shortlist

**Audience/problem:** students know famous names but need a useful way to choose what to investigate. **Idea:** three to five companies through one clear lens, each with a different supported reason to care.

**Story:** audience + selection criterion → name and reason for each company → saveable recap → one next action. **Visual language:** a consistent company/reason hierarchy, varied compositions and a final comparison. Use the [shortlist and ranking recipes](career-content.md). **Product:** JJIntern helps the next discovery step, without implying every featured role is listed. **Failure:** logo parade, generic praise, mixed-market salary rankings or calling an editorial selection the objectively best employers. A countdown needs reasons; a ranked claim also needs comparable evidence.
