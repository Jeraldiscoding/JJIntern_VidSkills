Revision 09 — capture choreography and motion critique
17 October 2026. Reviewer: claims_qa agent.

DESIGN INTENT
The new request needs a substantial change in what the audience sees, not a small increase in background speed. Revision 08 moved the packet only 370 screen pixels over five seconds. Faster-looking streaks could not make that packet trajectory feel decisive. The next version needs a visible launch and travel across the frame, followed by a capture that changes the graphic construction rather than merely consuming a rectangle.

SELECTED FLIGHT TREATMENT
Production lead is implementing two rightward passes linked by a short directional whip: pass one 3.100–5.150, x 70 to 1080; whip 5.150–5.320; pass two 5.320–7.100, entering from the left and reaching the receiver mouth at x 680. This is an editorial match-on-action transition, not a claim of one continuous camera trajectory. The much greater visible travel is the right priority.

Two risks need actual sequence inspection. First, avoid a dead interval after the entire packet has left the right edge: cut as its trailing edge exits, or carry the wake through the whip. Second, the incoming pass must already have momentum; do not restart with a slow ease-in that makes the packet appear to launch again. A strong or near-linear initial portion of pass two will better preserve energy across the transition. Keep the movement rightward on both sides of the edit.

CAPTURE CHOREOGRAPHY
1. Anticipate. Upper and lower receiver shell halves separate shortly before contact. The original logo stays fixed on an independent opaque center plane. The opening is visible before the packet reaches it.
2. Transfer. As the packet crosses the aperture, its three existing information bars extend into three flat ribbons. Use their established colors and slightly stagger their timing; this preserves the relationship between contents and result. The packet boundary progressively disappears behind the aperture. The ribbons visibly enter and route behind the opaque center plane, so they cannot bleed through the transparent gaps in the original logo.
3. Clamp. Close the jaws only after the final ribbon has cleared. Use one restrained 4–6 px overshoot and one thin rectangular pressure outline expanding approximately 20 px and disappearing in about 0.18 seconds. Avoid circles, particle bursts, confetti and layered impact decorations. The original mark must stay sharp, intact and undeformed.
4. Resolve. Remove the shell and take the framing to the centered mark. Make this a decisive final punctuation rather than a long crossfade. Stable logo and text holds follow the action.

Production timing now targets contact at 7.100, capture through 7.600, clamp 7.600–7.820, and brand takeover 7.820–8.800. This ordering gives the clamp a reason and makes the receiving action one readable event. The audio should support three distinct beats: launch, ribbon transfer and clamp. Preserve the isolated source ding before the drums enter.

TASTE PRIORITIES
The statement should come from coordinated motion and contrast: a clear source, rapid travel, a designed capture, then stillness. More decorative effects would weaken this. Keep a sharp main packet silhouette, use background streaks as supporting travel cues, and make the three ribbons inherit the bars rather than appear as an unrelated special effect. The receiver shell can displace; the original logo cannot squash, redraw or change color.
