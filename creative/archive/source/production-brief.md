# JJIntern — Your next move

Selected 17 October 2026. Audience: Singapore students exploring finance and technology internships. Format: a 20-second 16:9 portfolio brand film, 1920×1080 at 60 fps. Desired action: explore JJIntern.com. Abstract motion graphics, not an application demonstration.

## Mechanisms considered before animation

1. **Noise becomes a path — selected.** Orbiting career disciplines collapse into a continuous blue trajectory; the path becomes a sculptural ribbon, then opens toward the final invitation. Opening question “Too many directions?” resolves with “Find your next move.” The change is understandable without a feature claim. It supports distinct typographic, spatial, light and dark compositions.
2. **A browser search becomes an answer.** Use the genuine tested company-directory capture. Strong functional proof, but the existing workspace rough already uses that mechanism. A screen-centred tutorial gives less room for the requested motion-design showreel. Discarded for this film.
3. **Offer-letter / alert cascade.** An inbox explodes into employer offers or instant Telegram notifications. Visually dramatic but depends on unsupported functionality and hiring outcomes. Rejected entirely; no such states are depicted.

## Visual and musical direction

A continuous blue ribbon is the metaphor for a student finding direction. Space, depth, oversized sans type, an occasional italic serif and precise editorial framing replace slide cards. White sections make the directional change visible; black sections make the final destination feel resolved. Proposed campaign treatment uses authentic JJIntern palette, system sans and the supplied Instrument Serif 400 italic. The original raster mark is used at modest size and never traced or recoloured. Typography around it is editorial copy, not a newly claimed official wordmark.

Music is newly composed at 120 BPM, 4/4, 10 bars, exactly 20 seconds. Oscillators, envelopes, noise and calculated filters synthesize every instrument and effect. No samples, stock loops, generated speech or externally sourced audio.
